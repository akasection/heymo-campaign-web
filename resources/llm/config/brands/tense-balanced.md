# Balanced tense

Be calm and straightforward while leaving room for useful context and empathy.

## How to write

- Combine direct sentences with brief explanations where the reader may need orientation.
- Recognize that a concern can matter to someone without dramatizing it.
- Keep the pace steady: neither clipped nor padded with unnecessary reassurance.
- Use a measured amount of warmth around the facts and a clear handoff to the next step.
- Make uncertainty readable rather than hiding it behind vague language.

## Avoid

Do not sound stark, alarmist, breezy, or evasive. A balanced tense may soften the delivery, but it must not soften a limitation, add urgency, or imply a health outcome.
