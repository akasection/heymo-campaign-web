# Campaign writing rules

## Sequence shape

The three messages carry one job each and build in order without restarting the
conversation:

1. `promise` - confirm the visitor is in the right conversation and restate the Angle's
   single promise in the visitor's language. Do not expand the promise.
2. `mechanism` - explain the supplied, approved mechanism in plain, conservative terms.
   Do not add a health fact or interpret a result.
3. `objection` - address the recorded Angle-specific objection. Do not invent a new
   objection, solution, guarantee, or outcome.

## Style

### General rules

- Write around 100-300 words per email. This is not a hard limit, but work around it. You
  can go beyond 300 words if the conditions require it, but do not go below approx. 100
  words.
- Write for the supplied `presentation_profile` (register, pacing, and reassurance level).
- Use the visitor's stated sub-interest, trigger, and concern to change emphasis and
  framing only. Never convert free-text into medical interpretation.
- Greet with the preferred name once, in the first message, as a direct greeting token.
  You may omit the name there if omitting it better matches the Brand's voice and Angle's
  tone. Do not mention the name again in later messages.
- Never mention the visitor's age group, sex, or the demographic presentation rule in
  the email.
- Follow the supplied Brand voice and reading level whenever they do not conflict with a
  safety rule.
- When the Brand's reading level and the `presentation_profile` pull in different
  directions, keep the Brand's vocabulary level while following the presentation profile
  for pacing, reassurance, and visual treatment.

### Email 1: Promise

- Subject: Follows the Brand's voice and the Angle's tone, and may reference the topic
  described in the supplied Angle context (its audience or single promise), e.g.
  "Thank you for taking this first step with us."
- Always thank the visitor for their interest. The way you thank them should be in line
  with the Brand's voice and the Angle's tone.
- Prefer warm, positive phrasing and avoid "We do not" or other negative wording, except
  where a safety, accuracy, or compliance qualification must be stated plainly.
- Tells the visitor what information they were just shared with us.
- Close with wordings that encourage the visitor to stay in tune with the next email.

### Email 2: Mechanism

- Subject: Follows the voice, tone and other properties. It may or may not reference the
  topic described in the supplied Angle context (for example, the audience or single
  promise).
- Greet the visitor briefly. Match the tone and voice of the Brand and Angle.
- Mechanism email is a follow-up email of Email 1: Promise - so it's best to continue the
  conversation from Email 1 to this email. Avoid making this email sound disconnected
  from Email 1. Reuse the context or information.
- Since we are a campaign tool for US direct-to-consumer blood testing, try to inform the
  visitor that we involve blood testing in the process, matching the tone and voice of
  the Brand and Angle.
- Close with wordings that wish the visitor the best.

### Email 3: Objection

- Subject: Make it sound like a follow-up email. Do not include the angle/campaign name
  again here. Avoid negative phrasing unless accuracy or the recorded objection requires
  it.
- Greet and thank the visitor briefly.
- Make this email look like an extra sequence: an email that provides extra information
  to the visitor, not just like a "continuation".
- Address the recorded Angle-specific objection. Do not invent a new objection, solution,
  guarantee, or outcome.
- Try to avoid using negated wordings/negative phrases, unless it's a hard fact or
  requirement to prevent ambiguity.
- Use positive reinforcement optimally. Try to not be like a toxic-positivity advocate.
- Close with thanks again to appreciate the visitor's trust and interest in the campaign.
- Optionally wish the visitor to stay healthy when the tone and voice matches.
